# Secondary observational check (specified before inspecting trajectory outcomes)

Question: can the largest *sampled* closing displacement exceed the closing displacement at a common near-stop even with one uninterrupted pair of real, same-lane trajectories? No ramp model fitting; no estimate of road safety or reconstruction of emergency braking.

Use the first 15-minute eastbound I-80 recording from the US DOT NGSIM project. Pair passenger cars using follower's preceding ID and leader's following ID. Require constant IDs and lane, synchronised 0.1 s samples, and positive bumper clearance throughout. Use each follower's first qualifying fixed five-second approach followed by one second of common near-stop (both speed readings at most 0.5 m/s at all 11 samples). Both must be moving at least 1 m/s at the beginning of the approach. Treat the interval ending at the first second of a common near-stop as one observation; do not optimize for size of closure. Compare max(0, maximum sampled decrease in gap) against max(0, final sampled decrease in gap). The nominal endpoint is the last near-stop sample. Compare with a rolling five-sample median gap and with measuring the endpoint at the first near-stop sample. Report all eligible followers including zero discrepancies; no imputation across gaps or lane changes. Report descriptive counts above 1 m and maximum in meters. These ordinary, potentially nonmonotone car-following approaches and noisy video-derived positions do not validate the brake ramp, deceleration cap, or dry-road reliability. A second when both vehicles are nearly stationary is an operational proxy for stopping, not proof of a terminal stop.
